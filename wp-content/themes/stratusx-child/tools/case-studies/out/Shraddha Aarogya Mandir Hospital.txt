[SUBTITLE] **– THE HOSPITAL**
[H2] A Day Built on Paper
[P] For years, every patient visit meant another sheet in a folder. The folders filled cabinets, and someone had to walk over and pull the right one by hand each time a doctor asked.
[P] That worked when the hospital was smaller. At 150 beds and 12 departments, it stopped working. The file a doctor needed was often upstairs, or in use somewhere else, and the whole visit sat there waiting for it.
[SUBTITLE] **- WHERE THE DAY SLOWED**
[H2] Four Places the Paper Caught
[P] Nothing here was dramatic. These were four small delays that happened over and over, every day.

        **01 - Records Out of Reach**
        Pulling a paper file took minutes, and now and then it just could not be found. So a doctor either stood waiting, or re-ran a test whose result was already sitting in that same folder.
    [row 0 cell 1]
        **02 - Billing Left for the End**
        At discharge, someone added up the charges by hand off a pile of slips. The patient was ready to go home and still had to wait at the counter while that total got built.
    [row 1 cell 0]
        **03 - Pharmacy Running Blind**
        Stock and dispensing sat in different registers. A medicine could run low and nobody knew until it was gone, and what went out did not always line up with what the bill said.
    [row 1 cell 1]
        **04 - Numbers No One Could See**
        Occupancy and revenue were kept in registers. Ask how last week went and you waited days, because someone had to count it all up first.
